USB-C breakout pair, four pads (G, V, D+, D-). Remove the female board's two 5.1k Rd resistors (CC to GND) and any CC resistor on the male. The grip fits the well one way up only; most female boards tie both D+ and both D- contacts together, so check both plug orientations with the bare male board before you close the grip. Never plug either half into a real USB port or charger.

Print and assembly notes

  1. Print each rest bottom-down, exactly as it sits on the desk: the pocket opens upward and the channel and pockets bridge, so no supports are needed.
  2. Every dock and grip STL loads in its print orientation; print it as it loads.
  3. Magnetic pogo dock: print it upside down: top face on the bed (180 deg about X); no supports; PETG recommended for the snap blades.
  4. Magnetic: the rest can be slid away, the plug lets go at a few newtons.
  5. Press the keyboard half into the dock pocket (pin 1 = SDA at the marked -X end).
  6. The plug's two magnets sit closer to the pad than TP_METAL_KEEPOUT: check the cursor for drift with the plug docked (the IQS550 recalibrates at power-up).
  7. USB-C dock: print it upside down: top face on the bed (180 deg about X); no supports; PETG recommended for the snap blades.
  8. Never plug either half into a real USB port or charger: V carries the Pico's 3V3. Label both parts.
  9. Remove both 5.1k Rd resistors (CC to GND) from the female board, and any 56k / 5.1k CC resistor from the male.
  10. Keyboard end: Pico 3V3 → Schottky (1N5817 / SS14, anode to the Pico) → V pad; D+ = SDA (GP2), D- = SCL (GP3), G = GND. No RDY (the firmware polls).
  11. Female board: check that every receptacle shell leg is soldered, solder the 4 wires (and the diode), route them out of the rear channel, press the board in from the top (the shell slides down between the window's crush ribs), snap the dock into the plate, then press the retainer in and drive its 2 x M2x4 screws: it holds the board down, presses the shell, closes the window and its two prongs lock the back clips.
  12. To take the dock out: undo the 2 retainer screws and lever the retainer up at the pry notches first (it locks the clips).
  13. Deburr the dock's well bottom and window floor (printed bridges) and check the bare boards' fit before soldering. Measure the OLED cover: the dock's back face is 0.6 mm in front of its front edge and its top 0.2 mm below its underside (taken as 9.2 mm above the plate), so the dock still seats if the edge sits further forward unless the underside is lower.
  14. Grip: solder each wire on the front half of its pad and fan the 4 wires toward the middle over the push rib. Mix 2-part epoxy, fill the key groove around the plug shell and run a bead where the shell meets the board (keep it off the exposed shell), lay the board in the bottom half (rear edge on the push rib), lead the wires over the rib and down the slot, add the top half on the pegs and drive 2 x M2x4 screws from below (they crush the clamp ribs). Let it cure before the first plug-in.
  15. Plugging in: hold the keyboard (USB-C needs 5-20 N). Unplugging: brace a fingertip on the dock face beside the well and pull the grip - never the wires, never the rest.
  16. Leave the 12 mm wire slack as an S-loop in the plug bay: the grip comes back 10.65 mm before the plug clears the well.
  17. Retainer: print it upside down (top plate on the bed). Grip: the lower half on its bottom face, the upper half on its flat split face. PETG, no supports.
  18. PLA or PETG. Never use carbon- or metal-filled (conductive) filament: it detunes the capacitive pad.
  19. Part masses list the generator's infill estimates and the 100 % solid figure.
  20. Ballast bars (78 × 20 × 5 mm steel) sit at least 20 mm from the trackpad. Keep loose steel, magnets and coins away from the pad.
  21. Glass variant: the 1.1 mm glass bonds to the module's own adhesive. Seat the module from the top and never press on the glass edges; push it out from below through the Ø6 mm hole.
  22. Stick 6 Ø10 mm silicone bumpers into the 0.6 mm recesses. Make them 0.6 mm taller than the keyboard's, so the rest's top sits level with the switchplate.
  23. The TPS65 runs on 3.3 V only (VDDHI 1.65–3.6 V). Never feed it 5 V.
